HOW THE DAY RUNS

LIGHT DECIDES THE ORDER

Food first while the kitchen is calm and the grill is clean, exterior last when the truck lights beat the sky. Blue hour is the one slot that cannot move — it lasts about 25 minutes, so everything before it is timed to protect it.

0:0030 MIN

Set up and clean down

Wipe the serving window and counter edges, kill stray tape and stickers, lay props by color, tape a white card inside the window for bounce. Test flash against the truck's own signage.

0:3090 MIN

Hero food on the counter

Shots 01–05. Kitchen builds one dish at a time; shoot each within 90 seconds of the pass. Two builds per item — the first is a rehearsal, the second is the frame.

2:0060 MIN

Hands, griddle and the pass

Shots 06–08 plus reels R1 and R2. Crew in brand tees, no watches, clean hands. Shoot the smash sequence at least four times — the sizzle only reads once the griddle is right.

3:0045 MIN

Dee'serts block

Shot 09 and reel R3. Desserts arrive cold and go back cold — shoot this in one tight block, not spread through the day.

3:4545 MIN

Reset, eat, reload cards

Back up everything shot so far to two drives. Re-dress the truck for evening: lights on, menu board lit, bin emptied, floor swept.

4:3060 MIN

Blue hour exterior

Shots 10–12 and reel R4. Start 20 minutes before sunset and keep shooting past it. This is the block that sells the brand — protect it.

5:3030 MIN

Queue, crowd and buffer

Real customers if the truck is trading, friends in frame if not. Also the catch-all for anything the light or the kitchen stole earlier.

BEFORE YOU DRIVE OUT

KIT, PROPS AND THE RULES

Kit

  • Two speedlights — one on camera bare for the flash look, one off to backlight steam
  • 35mm and 50mm — the whole list shoots on these two
  • Tripod for shots 10 and R4
  • White and black cards — bounce inside the window, negative fill outside
  • Spray bottle (water + glycerin) for drink condensation
  • Two drives — back up at the 3:45 reset, not at the end

Props, in brand color

  • Butter yellow trays and background cards
  • Ketchup red paper wraps and sauce cups
  • Carbon counter surface — the truck's own steel works if clean
  • Branded napkins, one loud sticker per frame — never two
  • Crew in plain black tees, no watches, short nails
  • Nothing on the counter that is not in the six-color palette

The three rules that hold the look together

  • Hard flash, always front. Direct on-camera flash is the signature. If a frame looks soft and pretty, it is off brand — put the light back on the lens.
  • True color, no grading toward orange. Set a grey card at the top of each block. The palette does the work; the grade should not invent a mood.
  • One chaotic element per frame. A drip, a crumb, a bitten edge, spilled fries. One. Two reads as a mess, none reads as a stock photo.

Once the truck photos land

These prompts describe a generic black-and-yellow truck because I have not seen yours yet — the photos did not come through with your message. Send them and I will rewrite every exterior prompt around the real vehicle: its actual color, window position, signage placement and the surface it parks on.

They also unlock the better Midjourney method: paste a photo URL of the truck at the front of the prompt as an image reference, or use --sref with a frame you like, so the generated boards show your truck instead of a stand-in. That turns this from a mood board into a genuine pre-visualisation the crew can match on the day.
