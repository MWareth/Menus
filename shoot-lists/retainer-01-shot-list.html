THE LOCATION

WHAT THE TRUCK ALREADY GIVES US

Everything below is read off your photos. This is not a gritty street corner — it is a warm, green, family park at night, and that is a better and more ownable picture than the one I had assumed.

Five things that change the plan

  • The truck is butter yellow, not black. Every exterior prompt I wrote before this described a matte black truck. All of them are rewritten below.
  • It is lit from the outside in. The rope light and roof sign are on all night, so there is no "lights on" reveal to shoot — the drama has to come from the sky going dark behind them instead.
  • Blue hour is now the single most valuable slot of the day. Deep blue sky behind a yellow truck is a complementary contrast you cannot fake later. It lasts about 25 minutes.
  • The yellow body will poison your white balance. All that bounced yellow lands on the food. See the color note further down — this is the one thing that can quietly ruin the whole set.
  • The tagline ticker is a caption bank. Eleven lines already painted on the truck, in the brand's voice. The 8 designed assets should pull from it, not invent new copy.
HOW THE DAY RUNS

EVERYTHING BUILDS TO BLUE HOUR

Food first while the grill is clean and the light does not matter, then hands and griddle, then everything outdoors timed so the wides land in the 25 minutes when the sky is still blue and the truck is already glowing.

0:0030 MIN

Set up and clean down

Wipe the stainless ledge and the window glass, clear the boxes visible through it, sweep leaves off the turf in front, straighten the rope light. Shoot a grey card on the ledge and lock a custom white balance.

0:3090 MIN

Hero food on the ledge

Shots 01–05. Kitchen builds one dish at a time; shoot each within 90 seconds of the pass. Two builds per item — the first is a rehearsal, the second is the frame.

2:0060 MIN

Hands, griddle and the pass

Shots 06–08 plus reels R1 and R2. Crew in black tees, no watches, clean hands. Shoot the smash at least four times — the sizzle only reads once the griddle is right.

3:0045 MIN

Dee'serts block

Shot 09 and reel R3. Desserts arrive cold and go back cold — one tight block, not spread through the day.

3:4530 MIN

Reset and back up

Everything shot so far onto two drives. Re-dress: bin emptied, cables hidden, jack stand and bricks tidied, nothing red on the grass that is not ours.

4:1525 MIN

BLUE HOUR — do not move this

Shots 10–11 and reel R4. Start 20 minutes before sunset, keep shooting 20 minutes past it. Tripod up and framed before the window opens. This block sells the brand.

4:4050 MIN

Full dark — people and festoon

Shot 12 and the festoon wides. Once the sky is black the bulbs take over and the lawn reads as an event. Real customers if the truck is trading, friends if not.

5:3030 MIN

Buffer and pickups

Anything the kitchen or the light stole earlier. Never leave without shots 04, 07 and 10.

THE ONE THAT BITES

The yellow truck will wreck your white balance

Every surface on that truck is saturated yellow, and at night it is also a giant light source. Bounce off it lands on the food, and buns, cheese and cream all go jaundiced — the kind of thing nobody notices on the day and everybody notices in the edit.

Do three things. Shoot a grey card on the ledge at the top of every block and set a custom white balance, not auto. Gel the key flash with ½ CTO so it sits in the same warmth as the rope light instead of fighting it. And put a black card opposite the truck body to cut the yellow bounce on the shadow side of the food.

The exception is shot 04, the cut stack: black card behind, flash ungelled, clean neutral white balance. That one frame needs to be true color with no environment in it at all.

BEFORE YOU DRIVE OUT

KIT, PROPS AND THE RULES

Kit

  • Two speedlights — one on camera bare, one off to backlight steam
  • CTO gels — ½ cut, for the key. Non-negotiable on this location
  • Grey card — one per lighting block
  • 35mm and 50mm — the whole list shoots on these two
  • Tripod for shots 10, 11 and R4
  • Black and white cards — black kills the yellow bounce, white fills the window
  • Spray bottle (water + glycerin) for drink condensation
  • Two drives — back up at the 3:45 reset, not at the end

Dress the truck first

  • Clear the window. Cardboard boxes and the pink bag are visible in every frame
  • Hide the jack stand and bricks under the chassis, or shoot from the side that hides them
  • Sweep the turf — dry leaves and that red offcut on the grass
  • Polish the stainless ledge. It is the surface under every food shot
  • Crew in plain black tees, no watches, short nails
  • Nothing on the ledge that is not in the six-color palette

The three rules that hold the look together

  • Hard flash on the food, ambient on the place. Food gets direct front flash. The truck and the lawn keep their own warm light — do not flash the wides flat.
  • True color, no grading toward orange. The location is already warm. Grading warmer turns the whole set to soup; let the yellow do the work.
  • One chaotic element per frame. A drip, a crumb, a bitten edge. One. Two reads as a mess, none reads as stock.
