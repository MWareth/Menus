THE LOCATION

BORROWING THE TRUCK, NOT THE BRAND

These are Dee's desserts using Smash & Pass's pitch as the set. The truck's yellow glow and the festoon bulbs give warmth for free — but the frames stay Dee's: softer light, warmer surfaces, cream and brown rather than loud red and yellow.

HOW THE DAY RUNS

SHOOT IN ORDER OF HOW FAST IT DIES

Desserts are not burgers — some hold for an hour and some are finished in four minutes. Work through the stable ones while you dial in the light, then torch, assemble and shoot the fragile ones fast.

0:0030 MIN

Set up, polish, white balance

Polish the stainless ledge, clear the window behind it, lay out your surfaces. Shoot a grey card on the ledge and lock a custom white balance — do this again every time you move.

0:3060 MIN

The stable three

Shots 01–04 and 07–08. Lazy cake, brownies and the San Sebastian all hold their look for an hour. Use them to find your light and your angles before anything fragile comes out of the cool box.

1:3035 MIN

Torch and shoot the brûlée

Shots 05–06. Torch on site, shoot inside four minutes, then torch the next one. Bring at least four ramekins — a softened top cannot be rescued and you will want more than one crack.

2:0535 MIN

Build and shoot the pavlova

Shots 09–10. Assemble on location at the last second: meringue, then cream, then berries, then straight to camera. Two builds minimum.

2:4030 MIN

The spread

Shot 11. Re-plate everything fresh — do not reuse the pieces you have been handling for two hours. This is the frame that becomes the menu image.

3:1025 MIN

BLUE HOUR — the location frame

Shot 12. Framed and on the tripod before the sky turns. Deep blue behind a yellow truck lasts about 25 minutes and cannot be faked afterwards.

3:3525 MIN

Buffer and pickups

Anything that melted, wilted or went soft the first time. Never leave without 01, 05, 09, 11 and 12.

WHAT WILL GO WRONG

FIVE THINGS THAT RUIN DESSERT FRAMES

THE YELLOW CAST

The truck is a giant warm reflector. Meringue, cream and custard all drift yellow and it is invisible on the day. Custom white balance off a grey card every time you move, ½ CTO on the key so the flash matches the ambience instead of fighting it, and a black card opposite the truck to cut the bounce on the shadow side.

THE HEAT

Cream softens, meringue weeps, chocolate blooms. Everything lives in a cool box with ice packs and comes out one plate at a time, for one setup only. Nothing sits on the ledge waiting its turn.

CONDENSATION

Cold plates hitting warm night air fog and bead within a minute. Let plates come up to ambient before you dress them, or wipe the rim right before the frame — beads on a cream plate read as dirty, not fresh.

INSECTS

Sugar under lights outdoors at night. Keep a cloche or a clean cloth over anything not being shot, and check the frame at 100% before you move on — a fly on the pavlova kills the shot and you will not see it on the back screen.

THE FOUR-MINUTE TOP

A torched brûlée top goes from glass to sticky in minutes in humidity. Frame up, light it, and only then torch. Four ramekins minimum — treat each one as a single take.

BEFORE YOU DRIVE OUT

KIT AND SURFACES

Kit

  • Softbox or bounce — desserts are soft-lit; save the hard flash for burgers
  • ½ CTO gels and a grey card — both non-negotiable here
  • Blowtorch + at least four spare ramekins
  • Cool box with ice packs
  • 50mm macro for the singles, 35mm for the spread and the location frame
  • Tripod for shots 06, 10, 11 and 12
  • Tweezers for berries, soft brush for crumbs, small mirror for fill
  • Cloche or cloth — insects

Surfaces to bring

  • Dark slate board — for the lazy cake and brownies, holds the darks apart
  • Cream ceramic plates — Dee's palette, not white restaurant china
  • Weathered wood board — for overheads, warmer than steel
  • Black card — both a background and a yellow-bounce cutter
  • Use the truck's stainless ledge for anything where the location must read; use your own surfaces for the tight macros

The three rules for this set

  • Nobody in frame. No hands, no crew, no passers-by in the background of the wides. Every shot is a still life.
  • Soft light, hard shadow edge. Bounce the flash so the highlights roll, but keep one crisp shadow so it still looks shot at night and not lifted from a catalogue.
  • Whites stay white. Meringue, cream and ceramic are the reference. If they have gone warm, the frame is wrong no matter how good it looks.
